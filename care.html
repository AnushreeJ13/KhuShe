Understanding & Handling Molestation

What is Molestation?

Molestation refers to unwanted touching, groping, or any physical contact of a sexual nature without consent. It is a form of sexual assault and is never the victim's fault.

Warning Signs

  • Someone touching you in a way that makes you uncomfortable
  • Inappropriate comments about your body or appearance
  • Someone trying to get you alone or isolated from others
  • Being pressured to keep physical contact a secret
  • Someone consistently ignoring your personal boundaries

Safety Strategies

  • Trust your instincts - if something feels wrong, it probably is
  • Use clear, direct language: "Stop touching me," "Leave me alone"
  • Create distance between yourself and the person
  • Move to populated areas where others can see you
  • Use a confident posture and loud voice to assert yourself

Self-Defense Basics

The SING Method

If physically threatened, remember SING:

  • Solar plexus (strike to the middle of the torso)
  • Instep (stomp hard on the top of their foot)
  • Nose (strike upward on the nose)
  • Groin (knee or strike to the groin area)

Vulnerable Points

  • Eyes - use fingers to poke or gouge
  • Throat - strike with the side of your hand
  • Knees - kick to the side of the knee
  • Ears - cup hands and clap over both ears

Understanding & Responding to Molestation

How to Respond in the Moment

  • Say "NO" loudly and clearly. Use a firm voice to communicate that the behavior is unwanted.
  • Create distance between yourself and the person immediately if possible.
  • Draw attention by shouting for help or making noise to alert others nearby.
  • If in public, move toward crowded areas or seek help from authority figures.
  • Use self-defense techniques if necessary - aim for vulnerable areas.
  • Call emergency numbers (100 for Police, 1091 for Women's Helpline) as soon as possible.

After an Incident

  • Get to a safe location as quickly as possible.
  • Tell someone you trust about what happened.
  • Remember that it is not your fault - the perpetrator is responsible for their actions.
  • Consider reporting the incident to authorities.
  • Seek medical attention if needed.
  • Consider speaking with a counselor or therapist to process the emotional impact.

Prevention Tips

  • Stay in groups when possible, especially in unfamiliar places.
  • Be aware of your surroundings and avoid isolated areas.
  • Keep trusted contacts informed about your whereabouts.
  • Trust your instincts - if something feels wrong, remove yourself from the situation.
  • Consider taking self-defense classes to build confidence and skills.
  • Use your phone to share your location with trusted friends when going somewhere new.
  • Remember that setting boundaries is your right.

What Molestation Is NOT

  • Your fault, no matter what you were wearing or doing
  • A sign of your weakness or inability to protect yourself
  • Something to be ashamed of or kept secret
  • Ever justified or deserved, regardless of circumstances

What to Say When You Call

  • State your name and that you need immediate help

  • Stay on the line until help arrives if possible

Self-Defense Resources

Basic Self-Defense Techniques

  • The Hammer Strike: Using your fist with keys between your fingers, swing down with force
  • Heel Palm Strike: Strike upward forcefully with the heel of your palm under the attacker's nose
  • Elbow Strike: Use your elbow to strike backward into an attacker's face or body
  • Bear Hug Defense: If grabbed from behind, drop your weight and strike back with elbows or stomp on feet

Using Everyday Items

  • Keys: Hold between fingers to strengthen punches or use to strike sensitive areas
  • Pen/Pencil: Can be used to strike pressure points like the throat, temple, or eyes
  • Bag/Purse: Swing as a defensive weapon or use as a shield
  • Mobile Phone: Use edge to strike at pressure points or call for help

Verbal Self-Defense

Sometimes your voice is your strongest weapon:

  • Use a firm, loud voice to say "NO" or "STOP"
  • Yell specific commands like "CALL THE POLICE" (rather than just screaming)
  • Make noise - blow a whistle, activate a personal alarm, or shout
  • Create confusion by saying unexpected things to throw off an attacker
