Breast Cancer Awareness

Early Detection Saves Lives

Breast cancer is one of the most common cancers affecting women worldwide. When detected early, the chances of successful treatment are significantly higher.

This resource offers important information and tools for women in their reproductive years and perimenopause to understand, detect, and seek appropriate care for breast cancer.

Interactive Self-Check Guide

How to Perform a Breast Self-Examination

Regular self-examinations can help you become familiar with how your breasts normally look and feel, making it easier to notice any changes.

1
Look at your breasts in the mirror

Stand with your shoulders straight and arms on your hips. Look for any changes in size, shape, or color, as well as any visible distortion or swelling.

2
Raise your arms

Look for the same changes with your arms raised above your head.

3
Check for discharge

Look for any signs of fluid coming from one or both nipples (could be watery, milky, yellow, or bloody).

4
Feel your breasts while lying down

Use the pads of your fingers to feel all parts of the breast tissue, using a firm, smooth touch. Cover the entire breast from collarbone to the top of your abdomen, and from armpit to cleavage.

5
Feel your breasts while standing or sitting

Repeat the examination while standing or sitting, preferably in the shower when the skin is wet and slippery.

Set Monthly Self-Check Reminders

Enable monthly reminders

Important: If you notice any changes or abnormalities during your self-check, consult a healthcare provider promptly.

Recommended frequency: Once a month, preferably 3-5 days after your period ends. For women in perimenopause or post-menopause, choose a specific day each month.

Real Survivor Stories

Sarah's Journey - Diagnosed at 35

"I never thought breast cancer could happen to me at such a young age. Early detection saved my life. I found a lump during a self-exam and immediately went to my doctor. Five years later, I'm cancer-free and advocating for young women to take breast health seriously."

Michelle's Experience - Perimenopause and Diagnosis

"Going through perimenopause was already a challenging time with all the hormonal changes. When I received my breast cancer diagnosis, it felt overwhelming. What helped me was connecting with other survivors and focusing on one day at a time."

Lisa's Recovery - The Power of Support

"My journey through treatment showed me how important a support system is. From my family to my healthcare team to support groups, each person played a crucial role in my recovery. Now I volunteer to support newly diagnosed women."
