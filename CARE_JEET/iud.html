IUD (Intrauterine Device)

A long-acting, reversible contraceptive that's over 99% effective at preventing pregnancy.

About IUDs

What is an IUD?

An intrauterine device (IUD) is a small, T-shaped device that's inserted into the uterus to prevent pregnancy. It's one of the most effective forms of birth control available, with a failure rate of less than 1%.

There are two main types of IUDs:

  • Hormonal IUDs - Release progestin to prevent pregnancy (last 3-8 years depending on brand)
  • Copper IUDs - Use copper to prevent pregnancy (can last up to 10 years)

How effective is the IUD?

The IUD is one of the most effective birth control methods available:

  • More than 99% effective at preventing pregnancy
  • Works continuously for 3-10 years depending on type
  • No need to remember to take a pill or use protection
  • Works immediately after insertion (copper IUD) or within 7 days (hormonal IUD)

Less than 1 out of 100 women will get pregnant each year if they use an IUD.

What are the side effects of IUDs?

Side effects vary depending on whether you have a hormonal or copper IUD:

Hormonal IUD side effects may include:

  • Irregular bleeding and spotting (especially in the first 3-6 months)
  • Lighter periods or no periods at all
  • Mood changes
  • Breast tenderness
  • Acne

Copper IUD side effects may include:

  • Heavier, longer periods (especially in the first few months)
  • More painful cramps
  • Spotting between periods

Most side effects improve after the first few months. Serious complications are rare but can include perforation of the uterus or expulsion of the IUD.

What are the benefits of an IUD?

IUDs offer many advantages over other birth control methods:

  • Highly effective - Over 99% effective at preventing pregnancy
  • Long-lasting - Works for 3-10 years depending on type
  • Reversible - Fertility returns quickly after removal
  • Low maintenance - No daily or monthly tasks to remember
  • Discreet - No one can tell you're using it
  • Cost-effective - More affordable over time than other methods
  • Hormonal options - Can make periods lighter or stop them altogether

What are the disadvantages of an IUD?

While IUDs are safe for most people, there are some potential drawbacks:

  • Insertion can be uncomfortable - Some people experience pain during the procedure
  • Requires a healthcare provider - Must be inserted and removed by a professional
  • Possible side effects - Especially in the first few months
  • No STD protection - Doesn't protect against sexually transmitted infections
  • Rare but serious risks - Including perforation or expulsion
  • Upfront cost - Can be expensive without insurance (but cost-effective over time)

Who can use an IUD?

Most people can safely use an IUD, including:

  • People who have never been pregnant
  • People of all ages, including teenagers
  • People who want long-term but reversible birth control
  • People who want to avoid remembering to take a pill

IUDs may not be recommended for people with:

  • Certain uterine abnormalities
  • Active pelvic infections
  • Certain cancers
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding
  • Allergy to copper (for copper IUDs)

Talk to your healthcare provider to see if an IUD is right for you.
