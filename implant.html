Contraceptive Implant

A long-acting, reversible birth control method that's over 99% effective at preventing pregnancy.

About Contraceptive Implants

What is the contraceptive implant?

The contraceptive implant is a small, flexible plastic rod that's placed under the skin of your upper arm. It releases the hormone progestogen to prevent pregnancy and lasts for 3 years, though it can be taken out earlier if needed.

The implant is one of the most effective birth control methods available - more than 99% effective. That means fewer than 1 in 100 women who use the implant will get pregnant in a year.

How effective is the contraceptive implant?

The implant is more than 99% effective at preventing pregnancy. This makes it one of the most effective birth control methods available — even more effective than the pill, patch, or ring.

Once it's in place, you don't have to think about it again for up to 3 years. You don't have to remember to take a pill every day or get refills. And it works right away if it's inserted during the first 5 days of your period.

What side effects should I expect with the implant?

Some people may experience side effects from the implant, but these usually improve after a few months. Possible side effects include:

  • Changes to your periods (they may stop, become irregular, lighter, heavier, or longer)
  • Headaches
  • Acne
  • Mood changes
  • Breast tenderness
  • Weight gain

For most people, these side effects are mild and go away after a few months. If they don't go away or are bothering you, talk to your doctor or nurse.

What are the benefits of the contraceptive implant?

The contraceptive implant has many advantages:

  • Works for 3 years
  • More than 99% effective
  • Doesn't interrupt sex
  • Safe to use while breastfeeding
  • Can make periods lighter, less painful, or stop altogether
  • Can be taken out at any time by a doctor or nurse
  • Fertility returns quickly after removal
  • Doesn't contain estrogen

What are the disadvantages of the implant?

While the implant has many benefits, there are some potential drawbacks to consider:

  • Requires a minor procedure to insert and remove
  • May cause irregular bleeding or spotting, especially in the first 6-12 months
  • Doesn't protect against STIs
  • Possible side effects like headaches, acne, or mood changes
  • Can't be removed whenever you want - you need to see a doctor or nurse

How is the implant inserted and removed?

The implant is inserted by a trained doctor or nurse. Here's what to expect:

Insertion: The area on the inside of your upper arm is numbed with local anesthetic. The implant is then inserted just under your skin using a special applicator. The procedure takes just a few minutes.

Removal: The area is numbed again, and a small cut is made in your skin to remove the implant. This also takes just a few minutes. You can have a new implant inserted at the same time if you want to continue using this method.

After insertion, you'll be shown how to feel the implant to check it's in place. The implant can be removed at any time by a doctor or nurse if you want to get pregnant or switch to another method.
